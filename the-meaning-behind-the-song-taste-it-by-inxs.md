The hook that repeats “taste it” in INXS’s sultry mid‑80s single feels like a whispered dare, a promise, and a reminder all at once. Beneath the glossy production and Michael Hutchence’s unmistakable vocal swagger lies a tension between desire and restraint, a subtle battle between the intoxicating pull of the moment and the lingering after‑taste of regret. The song asks listeners to consider what it means to truly “taste” an experience—whether that be love, risk, or self‑destruction—and what is left when the first sip has faded. Because the track never spells out a concrete story, it becomes a canvas for personal reflection, making it an ideal candidate for deeper analysis.

## Key Takeaways
- **Desire as a consumable:** The chorus frames longing as something you can literally “taste,” suggesting both pleasure and the danger of over‑indulgence.  
- **Narrative of fleeting intimacy:** The verses hint at a brief, intense encounter that leaves the narrator yearning for more while fearing loss of control.  
- **Duality of empowerment and vulnerability:** Hutchence’s confident delivery masks an underlying fragility, showing how bravado can conceal insecurity.  
- **Symbolic use of sensory language:** References to flavor, heat, and consumption serve as metaphors for emotional saturation and the will to be fully felt.  
- **Production mirrors theme:** The crisp, metallic guitars and shimmering synths evoke a polished veneer that glints like the surface of a glass, beneath which turbulence simmers.  
- **Universal resonance:** Listeners connect with the song because it captures the paradox of wanting to be fully experienced yet fearing the inevitable emptiness that follows.  

### The Emotional Core of the Song  
At its heart, “Taste It” is a confession of yearning wrapped in confidence. Hutchine’s vocal tone oscillates between swaggering command and breathy intimacy, embodying a narrator who simultaneously **craves connection** and **guards against surrender**. The repeated invitation to “taste” something suggests a desire for sensory immersion—an urge to be absorbed rather than merely observed. Yet the repeated urging also becomes a plea for validation: if the other party truly tastes what is offered, the narrator’s significance is affirmed. The emotional tension is amplified by the lyrical ambiguity; the “it” could be romantic chemistry, a drug‑induced high, or even a fleeting moment of personal triumph. This fluidity allows the song to function as a mirror for anyone who has ever chased a high that felt both exhilarating and precarious.

### Main Themes and Message  
**Sensory Consumption** is the most prominent theme. By choosing the act of tasting—a fleeting, immediate sense experience—INXS frames desire as something consumable, temporary, and repeatable. This metaphor underlines the human impulse to **devour moments** before they dissolve. A secondary theme is **the paradox of control**: the narrator believes he can dictate the pace of the encounter by inviting the other to “taste,” yet the very act of invitation hints at surrender to an external force. There is also an undercurrent of **self‑reflection**, as the line about “learning the flavor of your own regret” (paraphrased) suggests that the narrator recognizes the inevitable bitterness that follows indulgence. The song ultimately asks listeners whether the fleeting pleasure of a taste outweighs the subsequent emptiness.

### Symbolism and Metaphors  
- **Taste / Flavor:** These words operate beyond the culinary realm; they signify *experience* and *memory*. A taste is recorded instantly but fades, mirroring how intense moments can linger as ghostly reminders.  
- **Heat / Fire:** The backdrop of a simmering rhythm suggests a smoldering passion, while the occasional sharp synth sting resembles a flash of flames—implying that desire can both warm and scorch.  
- **Glass / Surface:** The production’s polished sheen resembles a glassy surface, reflecting light yet concealing depth. This aligns with the lyrical idea that what we see is only a polished exterior; the true turmoil remains hidden beneath.  
- **Sipping vs. Gulping:** The contrast between a delicate sip and a ravenous gulp speaks to different approaches to intimacy—one that savors slowly versus one that devours in a single bite. The song leans toward the former, hinting at a willingness to linger rather than consume in haste.

### The Role of the Title and Hook in the Meaning  
The imperative “Taste It” works as both a command and a promise. As a command, it asserts dominance, implying that the narrator can dictate the terms of interaction. As a promise, it suggests that something worthwhile lies ahead, rewarding the listener’s participation. The repetitive nature of the hook mirrors the cyclical pattern of addiction—each repetition reinforces the craving, making the desire more insistent. By positioning the title as the central lyrical pivot, INXS underscores that the entire emotional arc revolves around that singular act of tasting, making the phrase a symbolic gateway to the song’s deeper psychological landscape.

### How Production and Sound Support the Narrative  
The track’s arrangement is a study in contrast, mirroring the song’s internal tug‑of‑war. The crisp, tight drum pattern provides a steady heartbeat, suggesting control and a measured pace—much like the controlled sip the narrator demands. Over this foundation, bright, jangly guitars add a tactile texture that feels almost tactile, evoking the physicality of taste. The synth pads float above, their ethereal sheen offering a sense of distance, as if the narrator is observing the encounter from a slightly removed perspective. This sonic layering creates a feeling of **polished allure masking restless undercurrents**, reinforcing the idea that the outward confidence conceals an inner vulnerability.

### Fan Interpretations and Why the Song Resonates  
Listeners often project their own experiences of fleeting romance or momentary highs onto the song, interpreting “Taste It” as an anthem for one‑night stands, impulsive decisions, or even the lure of fame. The universality stems from the way the track **captures the tension between immediacy and consequence**. Fans who have felt the rush of a new connection—or the sting of a brief indulgence—find the lyric’s ambiguity a perfect vessel for their own stories. Moreover, the catchy hook invites sing‑along participation, turning a personal confession into a communal chant, thereby reinforcing the shared human impulse to seek out sensations that feel both daring and fleeting.

## FAQ

**Q: What does the repeated phrase “taste it” actually refer to?**  
A: It functions as an open‑ended metaphor for any intense, sensory experience—romantic, drug‑related, or even a surge of confidence—that the narrator wants the listener to try and thereby validate.

**Q: Is the song about a specific relationship or a more general feeling?**  
A: Though the lyrics suggest an encounter with another person, the lack of concrete details keeps the narrative fluid, allowing it to address broader themes of desire, consumption, and the after‑effects of indulgence.

**Q: How does the musical style influence the lyrical meaning?**  
A: The sleek production and bright guitar tones create a façade of confidence that parallels the narrator’s outward bravado; the underlying synth swells hint at the unsettled emotions beneath that polished surface.

**Q: Why does the song feel both empowering and vulnerable at the same time?**  
A: Hutchence’s commanding vocal delivery conveys power, while the lyrical focus on tasting—an act of surrender—to someone else reveals an inherent vulnerability in seeking external validation.

**Q: Does the song comment on addiction, and if so, how?**  
A: Indirectly, yes. The cycle of urging another to taste, followed by hints of regret, mirrors the pattern of seeking a high, experiencing pleasure, and confronting the inevitable emptiness that follows.

**Q: What is the significance of the sensory language (taste, heat, flavor) used throughout?**  
A: These senses ground abstract emotions in tangible experiences, making the inner turmoil more relatable; they also emphasize the fleeting, consumable nature of the moments being described.

**Q: How might the song’s meaning change for listeners from different generations?**  
A: Older listeners may see it as a reflection on youthful excess and the dawning realization of its costs, while younger fans might interpret it as a modern anthem for living in the moment, illustrating the song’s timeless elasticity.
