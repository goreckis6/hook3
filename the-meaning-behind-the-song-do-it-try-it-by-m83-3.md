The rush of an electronic swell that arrives, pauses, then surges again feels like a breath caught in the throat of a decision that cannot be put off any longer. In M83’s **“Do It, Try It”**, that breath becomes a mantra, a pulse that pushes the narrator to confront the space between desire and action. The track does not simply ask a question; it insists on an answer, pulling listeners into the same crossroads where excitement, fear, and the weight of what‑if collide. Because the song’s phrasing is deliberately sparse, every synth line and reverberated vocal carries the emotional load, turning a simple exhortation into a meditation on agency, vulnerability, and the strange, almost sacred, terror of stepping into the unknown.

## Key Takeaways
- **Agency vs. hesitation** – the song dramatizes the internal battle between wanting to act and being paralyzed by doubt.  
- **Yearning for authenticity** – “Do it, try it” becomes a call to live genuinely, rejecting rehearsed versions of self.  
- **Risk as rebirth** – the act of trying is framed as a small death that clears the way for new possibility.  
- **Sound design mirrors emotional stakes** – layered synths and echoing vocals imitate the layers of hesitation and the eventual surge of resolve.  
- **Listeners hear their own indecisions** – the track’s open‑ended phrasing invites personal projection, making it a shared catharsis for anyone standing at a brink.  

### The Emotional Core of the Song

At the heart of **“Do It, Try It”** lies a narrator who feels the ache of unfulfilled potential. The verses are drenched in a kind of low‑grade anxiety, a humming undercurrent that suggests a mind circling a decision. This emotional texture is not expressed through concrete storytelling; rather, it manifests as a series of breath‑like synths that rise and fall, echoing the narrator’s own inhalations of hope and exhalations of doubt.

When the recurring hook arrives—“do it, try it”—it is delivered in a voice that sounds both distant and intimately close, as if the speaker is at once a friend offering encouragement and an inner critic urging caution. The duality creates a **tension between empowerment and self‑sabotage** that many listeners recognize from moments when they sense an opportunity but also feel the familiar sting of fear. The emotional arc progresses from **restlessness**, through **self‑questioning**, to a **quiet affirmation** that action, however tentative, is preferable to remaining static.

### Main Themes and Message

#### 1. The Imperative of Action

The song’s most obvious message is a *call to action*. The phrase “do it, try it” repeats like a mantra, stripping away any pretense of indecision. In a world saturated with endless options, the track argues that waiting for the perfect moment is itself a form of inaction. This theme resonates especially in a digital age where choices are too many and the fear of making the wrong one is amplified.

#### 2. Authenticity Through Experimentation

M83 often frames experimentation as a route to truth, and this track follows that pattern. By urging the listener to *try* rather than merely *do*, the song underscores that authenticity is discovered through the willingness to fail, to stumble, and to learn. The subtle shift from “do” to “try” implies a *process* rather than a finality, reminding us that the *journey* is as critical as the destination.

#### 3. The Paradox of Fear and Freedom

There is an undercurrent of **paradoxical freedom**: the very fear that immobilizes us is also a sign that something truly matters. The music swells whenever the lyrical hook repeats, suggesting that the fear itself is an indicator of significance. By confronting that fear, the narrator learns that liberation does not come from the absence of anxiety but from the decision to move forward despite it.

#### 4. The Small Death of the Old Self

A less overt, but powerful, motif is the notion of *dying* the former version of oneself. The act of trying forces the narrator to shed pre‑existing narratives—those of “I can’t,” “I’m not ready,” “It’s too risky.” This symbolic death is echoed in the track’s production, where layers of sound dissolve and re‑emerge, representing the dismantling of old identities and the birth of a more experimental self.

### Symbolism and Metaphors

#### The Repeating Hook as a Pulse

The constant repetition of the hook functions as a **heartbeat**. It is both a metronome for the song’s structure and a symbolic reminder that life’s most crucial moments beat in a steady, relentless rhythm. By aligning the listener’s listening experience with this pulse, M83 encourages us to synchronize our own decisions with the natural cadence of opportunity.

#### Synth Swells as Emotional Peaks

The synth lines that crescendo after each vocal phrase symbolize **emotional peaks**—the moments when the desire to act overwhelms hesitation. When the sound recedes, it mirrors the subsequent doubt that follows every daring act. This push‑and‑pull mimics the internal roller coaster of anyone who has ever taken a leap.

#### Echoing Vocals as Self‑Reflection

The layered, echoing vocal tracks act as a form of **self‑dialogue**. The echo suggests that the narrator’s thoughts are reverberating back at them, an internal conversation looping over the same question. This auditory metaphor captures the way we often spin the same question in our heads, waiting for the echo to provide a satisfying answer.

#### Light and Dark Textures

Throughout the track, bright, shimmering synths contrast with darker, more muted tones. This juxtaposition illustrates the **dual nature of risk**: the light represents potential reward and excitement, while the dark hints at the unknown consequences. By weaving both textures together, M83 paints a sonic picture of the complexity inherent in any decision.

### The Role of the Title and Hook

The title **“Do It, Try It”** functions on several levels. On a surface level, it is a straightforward imperative. Subtly, it also **mirrors the song’s structural rhythm**: two short phrases separated by a pause, much like the synth stabs that flank each vocal line. The title’s split nature suggests a **dual process**—first the action (“do it”), then the experimentation (“try it”). This order implies that the act of doing is not final; it demands continuous testing, reinforcing the message that authentic living requires ongoing effort.

The hook itself, delivered in a slightly breathy, almost spoken cadence, feels like a whispered dare. Its simplicity makes it **universally applicable**; each listener can attach their own context—starting a new relationship, launching a creative project, confronting a personal truth—turning the phrase into a personal pledge rather than a generic slogan.

### Production as Narrative

M83’s reputation for lush, cinematic soundscapes is evident throughout **“Do It, Try It.”** The production choices are not merely aesthetic; they function as an additional narrator.

- **Layered Ambience:** The opening layers are thin, creating a sense of empty space that reflects the narrator’s internal void before any decision is made. As the track progresses, more layers stack, filling the sonic field and representing the accumulation of motivation and possibility.

- **Dynamic Swells:** The crescendos line up with lyrical peaks, reinforcing emotional moments. When the music swells, the listener feels an uplift, encouraging a sense of empowerment.

- **Reverb and Delay:** Applying heavy reverb to the vocal lines gives them a distant, perhaps even timeless quality, suggesting that the question of “do it, try it” is not just personal but archetypal.

- **Subtle Rhythmic Pulses:** The faint, almost imperceptible drum beats act like a **heartbeat** beneath the synths, grounding the ethereal elements and reminding us that every grand decision still occurs within a human body.

These production techniques shape the listener’s experience, turning the song into a **sonic narrative** where the listener can literally feel the protagonist’s evolution from hesitation to tentative resolve.

### Fan Resonance and Personal Projection

Because the lyrics are intentionally sparse, fans often fill the void with their own stories. Some hear a **creative struggle**—the pressure to release new music, to paint, to write—while others project a **relationship dilemma**, interpreting the hook as a plea to finally express love or to break away from an unhealthy bond. The universality of the central question—*Do I take the step?*—creates a communal listening space where each personal fear and hope is validated.

Online discussions frequently mention how the song’s crescendo feels like a **personal victory** when the listener finally decides to act on a long‑held wish. The emotional catharsis is amplified by the track’s ability to translate an abstract internal conflict into a tangible, audible experience. In this way, “Do It, Try It” functions not just as a piece of music but as a **mirror** for the audience’s own hesitation, encouraging them to see the value in trying, even when the outcome remains uncertain.

## FAQ

**Q: What does the repeated phrase “do it, try it” actually represent?**  
**A:** It is a distilled command that stands for *taking a first step* and *testing the waters*. The split into two parts highlights the process: an initial decisive action followed by a period of experimentation and learning.

**Q: Is the song about a specific kind of decision, like love or career?**  
**A:** The track purposely avoids specificity, allowing any significant crossroads—romantic, creative, professional, or personal—to serve as the contextual backdrop. Its power lies in that openness.

**Q: How does the production reinforce the lyrical meaning?**  
**A:** Layers of synths build from empty to dense, mirroring the accumulation of courage. Reverb‑laden vocals echo the internal dialogue, while rhythmic pulses act like a heartbeat, grounding the emotional ascent.

**Q: Why does the song feel both hopeful and anxious at the same time?**  
**A:** The juxtaposition of bright, soaring synths with darker, muted tones creates a sonic representation of hope fighting anxiety. This duality aligns with the lyric’s theme that fear is an indicator of something worth pursuing.

**Q: Can “Do It, Try It” be interpreted as an anthem for self‑acceptance?**  
**A:** Absolutely. By urging listeners to act and then experiment, the song suggests that true acceptance emerges from experiencing one’s own choices, imperfections and all.

**Q: Does the song suggest that failure is inevitable?**  
**A:** Not inevitable, but *possible* and valuable. The emphasis on “trying” acknowledges that missteps are part of growth; the track celebrates the willingness to face them rather than guaranteeing flawless outcomes.

**Q: How does the title’s structure influence the listener’s perception?**  
**A:** The two‑part title creates a rhythmic echo that mirrors the song’s own cadence, reinforcing the concept of *dual steps*—action followed by exploration—thereby embedding the meaning directly into the listener’s mental framework.
