The chemistry between Lost Conduit’s brooding soundscapes and the vocal pairings of Luke Griffin and Greg Gilbert creates a space where inner turbulence can be examined without the usual weight of overt storytelling. “**Aversion**” feels like a whispered confession caught in the static of a late‑night broadcast—an invitation to sit with the uneasy feeling of watching a relationship, or even a part of oneself, slip away while the mind refuses to let go. That tension between longing for distance and fearing abandonment is the thread that pulls listeners back, demanding more than a surface‑level ear‑worm; it begs for a deeper excavation of what we push away and why.

## Key Takeaways
- **Aversion** portrays the paradox of desiring separation while being haunted by the memory of what once was.  
- The song’s narrator grapples with **self‑imposed exile**, using the language of love to describe a broader emotional withdrawal.  
- Metaphors of decay, water, and broken machinery illustrate the gradual erosion of identity and connection.  
- The title functions both as a psychological state and a lyrical refrain, cementing the song’s central conflict.  
- Sparse production choices, like the echoing synths and subdued percussion, mirror the emptiness that the narrator feels.  
- Fans resonate with the track because it captures the uncomfortable truth that sometimes the hardest person to forgive is ourselves.

## The Emotional Core of the Song
At its heart, “Aversion” is a study in **contradictory yearning**. The narrator’s voice, layered between Griffin’s plaintive tenor and Gilbert’s steadier baritone, embodies an internal dialogue. One side wishes to step back, to protect the self from further hurt; the other aches for the familiarity that is already slipping out of reach. This split is palpable in the verses, where the lyrical focus shifts from describing the physical environment—rooms that feel too large, streets that echo with silence—to an almost cinematic recollection of moments that once felt safe. The emotional palette is drenched in **melancholy**, yet it avoids self‑pity, opting instead for a quiet resignation that feels almost clinical.

The feeling of **fearful anticipation** runs throughout the track. Listeners hear a subtle tremor in the vocal delivery, as if each line is a breath held just before the inevitable exhale. That nervous energy becomes the song’s pulse, reinforcing the notion that aversion is not simply dislike, but an **involuntary, nervous reflex** to something once cherished. The bridge, where the melody briefly lifts, offers a fleeting glimpse of hope—a whisper that perhaps the distance can be bridged—only to be pulled back down by the weighty return of the refrain, reminding us that the emotional gravity is inescapable.

## Main Themes and Message
The most compelling theme in “Aversion” is **the self‑inflicted exile** that often follows betrayal or disappointment. Rather than pointing a finger outward, the song explores how we can become our own worst enemies, creating obstacles that keep us from healing. This is evident in the recurring image of closing doors that were once left ajar: a metaphor for the deliberate act of shutting off intimacy out of fear, not hatred.

Another layer is **the erosion of identity** under prolonged emotional strain. The narrator speaks of feeling “like a ghost in a mirror,” a line that suggests the loss of self-recognition when the emotional anchor that once defined us is undone. The message is simultaneously warning and solace: acknowledging that aversion is a protective mechanism can validate the listener’s own experiences without admonishing them for the resulting isolation.

Lastly, “Aversion” touches on the **paradox of desire and detachment**. The chorus repeatedly invites the listener to “stay away,” yet the very repetition implies an obsession with that distance, as if the act of naming the aversion keeps it alive. In this way, the song comments on how we sometimes *cling* to the very barriers we wish to dismantle, an insight that resonates profoundly with anyone who has struggled to let go.

## Symbolism and Metaphors
Lost Conduit populates “Aversion” with a rich visual lexicon that transforms intangible feelings into concrete objects. The most striking metaphor is the **rusted machinery** that threads through the verses. Mechanical decay stands in for emotional decline: gears that once turned smoothly now seize, oil that once lubricated now thickens. This imagery suggests that the relationship—or internal state—has been left unattended long enough for corrosion to set in, and that without intervention, the system will fail entirely.

Water, another recurring element, appears in the form of “flooded corridors” and “dripping ceilings.” Water can signify both cleansing and suffocation. In “Aversion,” the flooding represents emotions that have overflowed the capacity of the narrator’s inner walls, while the drip‑like percussion mimics the slow, inevitable wear of the psyche. The irony lies in the fact that this water is not cleansing but rather a **slow erosion**, subtly dissolving boundaries and reshaping the landscape of the self.

A third symbolic strand is the **broken mirror**. Mirrors traditionally reflect truth, but a cracked surface provides a fragmented, distorted view. This duality captures the fragmented self‑perception that results from ongoing aversion. The lyric that the narrator feels like a phantom when looking into the broken glass illustrates the disconnect between who they once were and the hollow silhouette they now inhabit.

## The Title and Hook in the Meaning
“**Aversion**” functions on two levels: as a noun defining the psychological repulsion and as a verb that commands action ("to avoid"). The repeated hook—"stay away" delivered in a sing‑song cadence—creates a paradoxical intimacy; the very act of issuing a command binds the speaker to the subject they wish to exclude. The hook’s simplicity allows it to become a mantra for listeners who have ever felt compelled to push away something they love, making the abstract concrete.

The title also invokes **psychological theory**, where aversion is a learned response to negative stimuli. In this context, the song can be read as an internal conditioning process: the narrator has, over time, associated closeness with pain, and now the mind automatically triggers avoidance. By naming this condition outright, Lost Conduit forces listeners to confront their own conditioned responses, turning the title into a mirror for self‑examination.

## Production as Narrative
The arrangement of “Aversion” is deliberate in its minimalism. Ambient synth pads hover like distant clouds, never fully resolving, mirroring the unsettled emotional landscape. The drum pattern is subdued, with a soft, almost brushed snare that provides a **heartbeat** rather than a driving rhythm, suggesting the song’s focus on an internal pulse instead of external momentum.

The mixing places Griffin’s higher register slightly forward, while Gilbert’s deeper tones linger in the background, a production choice that reflects the internal conflict between overt yearning and suppressed resignation. Reverb is used sparingly on certain vocal lines, creating an illusion of space that feels both intimate and distant, reinforcing the central notion of separation.

One of the most striking sonic choices is the **gradual build of static** toward the final chorus. The faint hiss rises beneath the melody, evoking the sound of an old radio losing signal—an auditory metaphor for deteriorating communication. When the voice finally returns clear, it feels like a momentary reconnection, only to be swallowed again by the static, underlining the cyclical nature of aversion.

## How Fans Interpret the Song and Why It Resonates
Listeners frequently report that “Aversion” feels like a **soundtrack to personal disconnection**—whether it be a romantic breakup, a severed friendship, or a period of self‑isolation. The ambiguity of the lyrics allows fans to map their own experiences onto the narrative, making the track a **personal canvas**. The concrete metaphors of rust, water, and broken mirrors provide tangible anchors that many can relate to, while the abstract emotional core remains universally accessible.

The track’s **raw honesty** without overt melodrama grants it credibility among fans who appreciate authenticity over theatricality. In a musical landscape saturated with hyper‑produced love anthems, “Aversion” stands out because it acknowledges the *painful* side of love—where loving someone can also mean loving the idea of protecting oneself from hurt. This duality makes it a **comforting paradox** for those who have struggled with the same internal tug‑of‑war.

## FAQ

**Q: What does “Aversion” actually describe—is it about a romantic relationship?**  
A: While the language can be read romantically, the song is intentionally vague. It captures any situation where emotional proximity becomes a source of anxiety, be it a romance, a friendship, or an internal dialogue with oneself.

**Q: Why does the narrator keep saying “stay away” if they clearly miss the other person?**  
A: The refrain is a manifestation of an internal defense mechanism. The repeated command represents a self‑protective impulse, a way to keep the pain at bay even though the underlying longing remains unresolved.

**Q: How do the production choices reinforce the song’s themes?**  
A: Sparse percussion mimics a hesitant heartbeat; echoing synths create a sense of distance; the rising static mimics deteriorating communication—all of which sonically embody the feelings of isolation and emotional decay described in the lyrics.

**Q: Are the references to rust and water purely literal?**  
A: No. Rust serves as a metaphor for neglect and corrosion of feelings, while water symbolizes both the overflow of emotions and the slow erosion of personal boundaries.

**Q: What is the significance of the broken mirror image?**  
A: The broken mirror illustrates fractured self‑identity. It suggests that after repeated emotional strain, the narrator no longer recognizes themselves, seeing only fragmented reflections.

**Q: Does “Aversion” suggest a path toward healing, or is it purely a lament?**  
A: Though predominantly a lament, the song’s moments of melodic uplift hint at the possibility of awareness. Recognizing the aversion is the first step toward breaking the cycle, making the track both a confession and a subtle call for introspection.

**Q: Why does the song resonate so strongly with fans despite its ambiguous lyrics?**  
A: Ambiguity invites personal projection. Listeners can apply the song’s metaphors to their own stories, turning a specific artistic expression into a universal emotional experience.
