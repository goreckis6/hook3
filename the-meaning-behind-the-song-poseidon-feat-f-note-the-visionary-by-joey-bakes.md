The moment “Poseidon” drops, the beat already feels like a tide pulling you under, but it’s the whispered confession that lingers in the background that makes the track impossible to ignore. Joey Bakes and his collaborator F Note The Visionary have managed to turn a club‑ready bass line into a confession of drowning in one’s own myth. The song asks: *what happens when the god of oceans—forever associated with power, depth, and inevitability—becomes a metaphor for a personal collapse?* It is a question that begs a deeper look, because the track is less about mythic grandeur than about the quiet terror of feeling endlessly submerged in a world you once commanded.

## Key Takeaways
- **“Poseidon” frames personal overwhelm as a mythic flood**, using the sea god as a symbol of both strength and suffocation.  
- The narrator’s voice oscillates between **defiant bravado and vulnerable pleading**, revealing a split between the public persona and private terror.  
- **Water imagery functions as a multi‑layered metaphor** for memory, addiction, and the relentless pressure of expectations.  
- Production choices—**low‑frequency rumble, echoing vocal reverbs, and intermittent silence**—mirror the push‑and‑pull of surf and undertow.  
- Fans connect with the track because it validates the experience of **feeling “god‑like” on the surface while drowning underneath**.  

### The Emotional Core of “Poseidon”

At its heart, “Poseidon” is a study in **contradictory emotional currents**. Joey Bakes sings from a place that feels simultaneously empowered and exhausted. The verses carry the swagger of someone who has likened himself to a deity, claiming control over the tides of his own life. Yet the pre‑chorus softens, letting a breathy vulnerability slip through the armor: a yearning for rescue that never truly arrives. The hook—repeating the name of the sea god—acts as a mantra, a way to anchor himself while he spirals downward. Listeners can sense a **fear of losing autonomy**, a dread that every triumph is merely another wave that might crash. The song channels that tremor of a king who knows his kingdom is built on sand, and the emotional tension lives in the space between the clenched fists of a ruler and the open hands of a beggar.

### Main Themes and Message

**Power versus impotence** is the most prominent theme. By invoking Poseidon, Joey Bakes juxtaposes divine authority with human frailty. He asks whether true power is owning the ocean or merely surviving its currents. This tension expands into a broader commentary on **mental health**, specifically how the pressures placed on young artists can feel like an unending sea of expectations. The lyric about “riding waves you never asked for” hints at the idea that success can become an unforgiving tide, dragging the individual farther from shore.

Another undercurrent is **identity deconstruction**. The featured verses from F Note The Visionary add a conversational layer, where the guest voice narrates an almost prophetic warning: that the sea will eventually claim everything it bears. This doubles as an **inner dialogue**, a part of the self that acknowledges the inevitability of decline. The song’s message, therefore, is not simply “don’t drown,” but **“recognize the water, learn to breathe in it, and negotiate with it.”** It’s a call to reframe vulnerability as a form of mastery rather than defeat.

### Symbolism and Metaphors

Water dominates the lyricism, but each reference carries a distinct sub‑meaning.  
- **Storms** are not just weather events; they echo inner turmoil, sudden bouts of anxiety that can appear without warning.  
- **Currents** represent the invisible forces—industry pressure, family expectations, personal demons—that push an individual in a direction they may not have chosen.  
- **The sea floor** functions as a metaphor for buried memories or suppressed emotions, a place the narrator fears to explore but knows must be confronted to avoid being lost in the surface turbulence.  

The title itself, **“Poseidon,”** is an obvious metaphor, yet its usage is *dual*. On one level, the god is a **guardian of the deep**, implying that the narrator is seeking protection from his own "sea." On another, Poseidon is also a **breaker of worlds**, suggesting the potential for the narrator to destroy his own constructs if the flood becomes too much to hold back. The hook’s repetition is like a lighthouse’s foghorn—reassuring yet reminding listeners of the looming danger.

### The Role of the Title and Hook in the Meaning

The chorus in “Poseidon” repeats the name of the deity with a melodic cadence that feels almost ritualistic. This repetition does more than cement the song’s hook; it **creates a cyclical structure** that mirrors the ocean’s relentless churn. By embedding the title within the refrain, Joey Bakes makes the mythological concept inseparable from his personal narrative. The hook, therefore, becomes a **psychological anchor** for both the artist and the listener—a word that can be whispered for comfort or shouted when surrendering to the tide.

Furthermore, the way the hook is delivered—half‑spoken, half‑sung—evokes a **chanting of a prayer**. It suggests that the narrator is both pleading to and confronting Poseidon, blurring the line between worshipper and deity. This duality reinforces the song’s theme that within each individual resides both the *storm‑maker* and the *storm‑taker*.

### How Production and Sound Support the Emotional Narrative

From the opening two bars, a low‑frequency rumble simulates the pull of an undercurrent, subtly encouraging the listener to feel the weight of water before any vocal appears. The **delayed snare hits** mimic distant waves, giving space for the verses to breathe. When F Note The Visionary’s verse arrives, the instrumental drops to a sparser texture, creating a sense of **sudden calm before a deeper plunge**—a sonic representation of a momentary respite from overwhelming pressure.

Reverb is applied not just to the vocals but also to the synth pads, making them feel as though they’re resonating from beneath the surface. In the bridge, there is a deliberate silence—a brief, almost deafening pause that mirrors the stillness one feels when submerged at depth, before the rush of a bubble of air. These production choices serve as an **auditory metaphor for ascent and descent**, reinforcing the lyrical imagery and ensuring that the emotional journey is felt as much as it is heard.

### Fan Interpretation and Resonance

Listeners often cite “Poseidon” as a **soundtrack for those moments when they feel out of control**, especially in high‑stakes environments like school, work, or the music industry itself. The way fans describe the track—“the song that feels like a deep‑sea dive in a club” or “the anthem for when my confidence is a wave that’s about to break”—highlights how the track validates the paradox of feeling powerful yet powerless. Many fans write about the song as a **cathartic release**, using the repeated invocation of Poseidon as a personal mantra to remind themselves that it’s okay to admit to being overwhelmed while still standing tall on the deck.

The collaboration with F Note The Visionary is also widely discussed. Fans interpret the guest’s verses as an **inner voice** that people often ignore, urging a closer look at the causes of their “flood.” This resonates because the song doesn’t merely point out a problem; it provides a *dialogue* between the self‑image and the suppressed self. Consequently, “Poseidon” functions as both a **warning and an invitation**, encouraging listeners to wade into their deeper emotions rather than merely skimming the surface.

## FAQ

**Q: Why does Joey Bakes choose a mythological figure instead of a more literal image?**  
A: The sea god embodies *scale* and *inexorability* that everyday language struggles to convey. By using Poseidon, he taps into a cultural reservoir of power and danger, which lets the personal struggle feel universal and timeless.

**Q: Is the song about actual water addiction or a metaphor for something else?**  
A: The water references operate on multiple levels. While some lines hint at substance use (the “drowning” feeling of dependency), most are metaphorical, pointing to emotional overload, societal pressure, and the feeling of being pulled by forces beyond one’s control.

**Q: How does the featured artist, F Note The Visionary, alter the song’s meaning?**  
A: F Note’s verse serves as an *internal dialogue*, a voice that acknowledges the impending crash while still offering an almost prophetic warning. This contrast deepens the narrative, showing that the protagonist isn’t alone in his turmoil.

**Q: Does the track suggest any form of resolution or hope?**  
A: The ending leaves the tide unresolved, mirroring the reality that many never find a clean “shore.” However, the repeated mantra of Poseidon can be read as a *ritual of acceptance*, implying that acknowledging the flood is a step toward learning how to navigate it.

**Q: In what ways do the production elements enhance the lyrical themes?**  
A: The low‑end rumble, strategic silences, and watery reverbs act as *sonic symbols* for depth, pressure, and the intimacy of being submerged. These choices turn the listening experience into a physical feeling of being under water.

**Q: Why has the song resonated particularly with younger audiences?**  
A: Younger listeners often feel the dichotomy of outward success versus internal pressure. “Poseidon” articulates that tension through a vivid, mythic lens, giving them a language to describe an otherwise ineffable experience.

**Q: Is there a hidden narrative across the entire album that “Poseidon” contributes to?**  
A: While the broader album context remains open, “Poseidon” can be seen as a pivotal moment where the protagonist confronts the deepest part of his psyche, suggesting a larger arc of confronting and reconciling with internal tides.
