The swell of Meredith Edgar’s “Tidal Waves” feels like a secret shared between the sea and the soul—an invitation to listen beyond the shimmer of synths and into the undercurrent of longing that pulls at the narrator’s chest. From the first swell of a reverberating bass that mimics a rolling ocean, the track sets up a paradox: the water that should cleanse is also the force that drowns. Listeners are instantly asked to confront the uneasy truth that the very emotions we chase for relief can become the tide that sweeps us under. This tension makes “Tidal Waves” ripe for a deeper dive, because the song’s poetic surface only hints at a complex navigation of love, loss, and self‑recognition.

## Key Takeaways
- **The song frames emotional overwhelm as a literal ocean**, using water imagery to map the narrator’s internal storm.  
- **Narrative perspective is intimate and confessional**, positioning the speaker as both victim and architect of their own surge.  
- **Metaphors of tides, currents, and erosion illustrate cycles of hope and resignation**, suggesting that healing is not linear.  
- **Production choices—reverb‑laden vocal washes, gradual crescendos, and low‑frequency pulses—mirror the push‑pull of a wave’s rise and crash.**  
- **Fans resonate with the track because it captures the paradox of craving connection while fearing its collapse.**  
- **The title acts as a double‑edged hook**, reminding listeners that every high can become a breaking point.  
- **Underlying themes of agency, surrender, and rebirth emerge through the interplay of lyrical ambiguity and sonic texture.**  

### The Emotional Core of “Tidal Waves”

At its heart, “Tidal Waves” is a confession of *simultaneous yearning and dread*. The narrator describes a relationship that feels like standing on a shore, watching the horizon glow with promise yet fearing the inevitable surge. This duality is evident in the way Meredith Edgar shifts from moments of fragile optimism—when the lyric‑voice speaks of light breaking through clouds—to sudden drops of anxiety, where the same water that carries her forward turns threatening. The emotional core, therefore, is **the tension between the desire to be carried away by love and the terror of being lost to it**. By framing this conflict in the language of the ocean, Edgar gives listeners a visceral way to feel that oscillation in their own bodies: the breath that holds before a wave, the sudden gasp when it crashes.

### Narrative Perspective: A Self‑Reflective Voice

The song’s narrative is delivered in the first person, but it isn’t a simple confession; it’s a dialogue with an unseen “other” that also represents the narrator’s inner tide. Throughout the verses, there is an implied conversation: the speaker both asks for rescue and insists on their own responsibility for the rising water. This internal debate is crucial because it allows the song to explore **agency versus surrender**. When the narrator mentions feeling pulled toward an unknown depth, the listener can interpret that as a partner’s pull, a personal trauma, or even an addictive pattern. By keeping the “other” ambiguous, Edgar invites a broad spectrum of self‑identification, allowing each listener to project their own tidal experience onto the track.

### Main Themes and Message

**1. The Cycle of Hope and Collapse** – The repeated imagery of waves that build, crest, and fall mirrors emotional cycles. Each chorus rises in intensity, offering a fleeting sense of triumph before the bridge drops back into a quieter, almost resigned tone. This structural pattern reflects how hope often feels like a surge that can’t be sustained without a counterbalancing pull.

**2. The Illusion of Control** – “Tidal Waves” repeatedly hints at trying to steer or dam the water, a futile effort that underscores the theme that some forces are beyond personal command. The lyric about building a sandcastle before the tide arrives illustrates how the narrator builds temporary defenses that inevitably dissolve.

**3. Rebirth Through Erosion** – While erosion traditionally connotes loss, Edgar subtly flips this notion: the narrator hints that being worn down can also mean being reshaped. The final instrumental swell suggests that after the crash, there is room for a new shoreline—a metaphor for personal growth after emotional upheaval.

### Symbolism and Metaphors

Water is the central metaphor, but it works on multiple layers:

- **Tidal Waves as Emotional Overload** – The sheer size and inevitability of a wave echo feelings that feel impossible to contain. When the song describes the “pull of the undertow,” it speaks to the subconscious forces that drag us toward old patterns, even when we consciously try to stay afloat.

- **Seafoam and Salt** – The references to foamy caps and salty air symbolize the aftermath of emotional exposure. Salt, a preservative, hints at how painful experiences can become protective memories, while foamy caps represent fleeting moments of clarity that burst quickly.

- **The Shoreline** – The recurring mention of standing on the sand positions the narrator at a liminal space: the boundary between safety (land) and danger (sea). This liminality captures the moment before a decision—whether to step into the water or stay ashore.

- **Ships and Lighthouses** – Though not explicit, the undercurrent of navigation imagery suggests a longing for guidance. The lighthouse, a beacon that slices through darkness, can be read as a metaphor for the narrator’s search for an inner compass amid chaotic feelings.

### The Role of the Title and Hook

The repetition of the phrase “tidal waves” in the chorus is more than a catchy hook; it is a **mantra that reinforces the song’s central tension**. By chanting the very phenomenon that both fascinates and frightens the narrator, Edgar gives the listener a structural anchor. Every time the phrase surfaces, the instrumentation swells, mimicking the physical feeling of a wave growing in size. The title, therefore, serves both as a literal description of the sonic landscape and as an emblem of the emotional tumult the song explores.

### Production Choices that Echo the Narrative

Meredith Edgar’s production on “Tidal Waves” is meticulous in its alignment with lyrical intent. The track opens with a low, rumbling sub‑bass that feels like the ocean’s swell far beneath the surface. As the verse progresses, layered vocal harmonies are drenched in reverb, creating a sense of space akin to standing on a vast shoreline. The pre‑chorus introduces a rising synth line that crescendos into the full‑throttle chorus, mirroring the heartbeat acceleration before a wave crashes.

Mid‑song, a brief breakdown strips away most instrumentation, leaving only a lone piano note that echoes like a lone buoy bobbing on still water. This sparse moment offers a breath, representing the eye of the storm—a fleeting calm before the emotional tide returns. The final outro contains a descending glissando that sinks lower in the mix, evoking the feeling of being pulled under. These production elements do more than complement the words; they *embody* the song’s central metaphor, allowing listeners to experience the tide physically through sound.

### Fan Resonance: Why Listeners Feel Seen

The track has quickly become a touchstone for fans who describe feeling “caught in the push‑pull of love and anxiety.” Many relate the water metaphor to specific life events—breakups that seem to happen in cycles, mental health struggles where moments of calm are abruptly shattered, or creative pursuits that feel like a relentless tide of inspiration and burnout. The openness of the lyrics—never naming a concrete scenario—means each listener can map their own experiences onto the waves.

Social media conversations often highlight how the song’s bridge, with its gentle, almost whispered tonality, feels like a personal confession whispered directly to the listener. Others point out the cathartic power of the final crescendo, saying it provides a sense of *release* that mirrors the act of finally surrendering to a wave after fighting it for too long. This shared emotional vocabulary shows that “Tidal Waves” operates as both a personal diary and a communal anthem for anyone who has ever felt the sea of their own feelings rise beyond control.

### A Closer Look at Lyrical Ambiguity

Meredith Edgar deliberately avoids naming a specific lover or exact event, opting instead for a series of vivid yet universal images. This lyrical ambiguity serves several functions: it protects the personal intimacy of the narrative, invites broader identification, and mirrors the unpredictable nature of water itself—always shifting, never fixed. By employing phrases that can denote both internal states (e.g., “the current inside me”) and external forces (e.g., “the tide pulling at the pier”), Edgar creates a lyrical double‑layer that reinforces the song’s theme of *interconnectedness between self and environment*.

The lack of concrete resolution—no definitive “we will stay together” or “I’m leaving” line—also underscores the thematic idea that some emotional tides simply recede without a tidy ending. The song ends on an unresolved harmonic tone, leaving listeners in a state of anticipation, much like a shore waiting for the next wave.

## FAQ

**Q: What does the ocean metaphor represent in “Tidal Waves”?**  
A: The ocean stands in for overwhelming emotions—particularly the cyclical nature of hope, fear, and longing. Waves illustrate how feelings build, crest, and crash, while the shoreline represents the tentative safety we cling to.

**Q: Is the song about a romantic relationship or something broader?**  
A: While romantic undertones are present, the lyric ambiguity allows the track to apply to any intense, recurring emotional pattern—be it mental health, creative burnout, or familial ties.

**Q: Why does the narrator seem both resistant and surrendering to the tide?**  
A: This duality reflects the central conflict of agency versus inevitability. The narrator wants to control the surge but ultimately acknowledges that some forces, like water, will shape us regardless of our resistance.

**Q: How does the production reinforce the lyrical themes?**  
A: Low‑frequency rumbles mimic a distant swell, reverb‑heavy vocals create a feeling of vast space, and the gradual build‑up to the chorus mirrors a wave’s rise, while the stripped‑down bridge offers a moment of calm before the next surge.

**Q: What is the significance of the song ending without a clear resolution?**  
A: The unresolved ending mirrors real life’s lack of tidy conclusions. It leaves the listener in a state of expectancy, echoing the continual arrival of new emotional tides.

**Q: Why do fans often reference the song when talking about mental health?**  
A: The imagery of being pulled under while yearning for the surface aligns closely with experiences of anxiety or depression, where relief feels fleeting and the next wave appears inevitable.

**Q: Does the title “Tidal Waves” serve a purpose beyond describing the sound?**  
A: Yes; the repeated hook embeds the metaphor in the listener’s memory, reinforcing the notion that each emotional surge is both a powerful force and a recurring pattern—much like the tide itself.
