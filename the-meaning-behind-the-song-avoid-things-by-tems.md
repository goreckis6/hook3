The moment “Avoid Things” first drifts through a speaker, there’s an unsettling feeling of quiet urgency. Tems’s voice, half‑whisper, half‑chant, creates a space where listeners are invited to stare into the under‑current of a relationship that feels simultaneously intimate and distant. The song isn’t just about a lover; it’s a meditation on the instinct to protect oneself from recurring hurt, the tension between yearning for connection and the habit of stepping back. This push‑and‑pull makes “Avoid Things” a fertile ground for deeper analysis, because every sigh, every breath‑yoked pause, holds a clue to the internal conflict that Tems is navigating. By pulling apart those clues, we can understand why the track feels like a therapeutic confession and why it resonates with anyone who has ever built emotional walls as a survival tactic.

## Key Takeaways
- **Self‑preservation vs. desire for intimacy** drives the narrative, showing how protective habits become a barrier to love.  
- The **song’s structure mirrors the cyclical nature of avoidance**, with verses that retreat and choruses that briefly open up.  
- **Metaphoric imagery of “things” and “spaces”** functions as stand‑ins for emotional triggers and safe zones.  
- Production choices—sparse drums, airy synths, and reverb‑drenched vocals—serve as an **aural embodiment of distance and yearning**.  
- Fans interpret the track as a **personal anthem for anyone learning to negotiate boundaries** while still craving closeness.  

### The Emotional Core of “Avoid Things”

At its heart, Tem

s is portraying a narrator who is **caught between longing and self‑defense**. The verses are saturated with a sense of resignation; the singer admits to pulling away whenever intimacy threatens to become too consuming. This isn’t self‑destruction—it’s a protective reflex honed by past experiences. The emotional palette is composed of **quiet anxiety, fragile hope, and a lingering sadness** that surfaces when the walls become too thick to scale. When the chorus lifts, there’s a fleeting release—a glimpse of what could be if the avoidance fell away. That contrast highlights the **internal tug‑of‑war** between the desire to be seen and the fear of being overwhelmed.

The fear expressed is not overt panic but rather a **subtle, almost breathless apprehension**. Tems's vocal timbre carries the weight of someone who has learned to read the warning signs before they manifest, and the lyricism paints a picture of someone constantly scanning the horizon for the next “thing” that might cause emotional turbulence. This scanning becomes a **habitual vigilance**, turning ordinary moments into potential threats, which in turn fuels a chronic state of emotional hyper‑awareness.

### Main Themes and Message

#### 1. **Boundaries as Both Shield and Prison**
One of the song’s most compelling messages is that boundaries, while essential for mental health, can also become **self‑imposed prisons** if they’re built from fear rather than clarity. The narrator’s repeated insistence on avoiding certain situations reveals a paradox: the very actions meant to protect her also isolate her from the very connections she craves.

#### 2. **The Cycle of Repetition**
The lyric structure suggests a loop—each stanza circles back to the same avoidance motif, implying that breaking the pattern isn’t straightforward. This mirrors real‑life cycles where individuals keep returning to familiar emotional scripts even when they recognize the harm they cause.

#### 3. **Self‑Recognition and Growth**
Although the song dwells in the realm of avoidance, there’s an undercurrent of **self‑recognition**. By naming the things she evades, the narrator is already taking the first step toward confronting them. The subtle shift in tone near the bridge hints at a tentative opening, a willingness to consider that avoidance might be a temporary coping mechanism rather than a permanent identity.

#### 4. **The Unspoken Language of Love**
Instead of pointing to overt romantic gestures, Tems emphasizes **the quieter language of presence and absence**. The spaces left unfilled become a dialogue of their own, speaking louder than any declaration. This theme resonates with listeners who have felt love’s weight in what is left unsaid.

### Symbolism and Metaphors

The title “Avoid Things” functions on multiple symbolic levels. **“Things”** are deliberately vague, turning everyday objects or moments into stand‑ins for emotional triggers—maybe a text message, a certain street, or a memory. By not specifying, Tems universalizes the experience; every listener can project their own “things” onto the canvas.

The recurring motif of **“rooms”** or **“spaces”** acts as a metaphor for mental compartments. Moving from one room to another reflects the internal shifting of focus—leaving behind a painful recollection to enter a safer mental territory. The occasional mention of **“doors”** that are left unopened underscores the **opportunity cost** of avoidance: each unopened door represents a potential relationship, an undiscovered joy, or a crucial self‑realization that remains inaccessible.

Another pervasive image is **“water”**, often used to signify fluidity and the ability to adapt. The narrator likens herself to a surface that refuses to be dipped fully, hinting at a **fear of being submerged** in emotional depth. The water metaphor also points to how avoidance can lead to **emotional drought**, where once‑lively currents become still and stagnant.

The **repeated sighs** embedded in the production act as an auditory metaphor for exhalation after a long‑held breath—signifying relief when the act of avoidance momentarily ceases. Conversely, the breathy pauses simulate the weight of **unspoken tension**, reinforcing the lyric’s subtext of suppressed yearning.

### The Role of the Title and Hook

The hook—“I just… avoid things”—is a lyrical mantra that both **reveals and conceals**. Its simplicity makes it instantly relatable, yet its ellipsis suggests an unfinished thought, a hesitation to fully articulate the underlying pain. By repeatedly returning to this fragmented phrase, Tems emphasizes the **habitual nature** of the behavior, making it feel like an ingrained reflex rather than a conscious decision.

The title also creates a **psychological distancing** for the listener; it invites them to step back and observe rather than jump straight into the emotional vortex. This mirrors the narrator’s own tactic of **observing from afar**—providing a meta‑commentary that the song itself is an act of avoidance, a safe way to explore a raw subject without fully immersing in it.

### How Production and Sound Support the Narrative

From the opening ambient hum to the soft, syncopated drum pattern, the production design feels like an **acoustic representation of interior space**. The airy synth pads float around the vocals like walls that both protect and isolate. When the chorus swells, additional layers of backing vocals create a sense of **collective yearning**, as if the internal monologue is suddenly being echoed by unseen observers.

The deliberate use of **reverb** on Tems’s voice elongates each word, giving the impression of thoughts reverberating within a cavernous mind. This effect underscores the **loneliness of avoidance**, where each decision echoes indefinitely, shaping the mental landscape.

Minimalistic basslines appear only during moments of introspection, grounding the track in a **subtle gravity** that mirrors the weight of the narrator’s self‑imposed barriers. The sparse arrangement ensures that no element overshadows the vocal, keeping the **emotional narrative front and center**, much like someone who keeps their emotional baggage close, revealing only what they deem necessary.

### Fan Interpretation and Why It Resonates

Listeners frequently describe “Avoid Things” as a **self‑care anthem**, a soundtrack for moments when they need to step back from overwhelming emotional demands. Many relate the song to situations where they have to protect themselves from toxic relationships, demanding jobs, or personal insecurities—places where avoidance is a survival tactic.

The vagueness of “things” allows fans to **project personal narratives** onto the track: some see “things” as past lovers, others as familial expectations, and still others as internal doubts. This elasticity makes the song a **blank canvas for emotional catharsis**, letting each listener find their own meaning while still feeling understood.

Moreover, the cultural context of Tems’s artistry—her reputation for authenticity and vulnerability—adds credibility to the lyrical content. Fans trust that the avoidance she sings about stems from lived experience rather than a manufactured trope, which deepens the emotional impact.

The track’s **ambient production** also contributes to its therapeutic quality. Many fans report using it for meditation, journaling, or as background music while they sit with their thoughts. The song’s soothing yet unsettled atmosphere mirrors the process of confronting avoidance: it feels **calm enough to listen to, but provocative enough to inspire inner dialogue**.

## FAQ

**Q: What does “avoid things” specifically refer to in the song?**  
A: The phrase is deliberately vague, acting as a placeholder for any emotional trigger—be it a person, memory, or situation—that the narrator perceives as potentially harmful. Its openness invites each listener to fill in the blank with their own “things.”

**Q: Is the song about a romantic relationship or broader emotional patterns?**  
A: While romantic overtones are present, the lyrical content and tonal shifts suggest a broader examination of self‑protective habits. It speaks to any context where an individual feels the need to step away to preserve their well‑being.

**Q: How does the production reinforce the theme of avoidance?**  
A: Sparse instrumentation, lingering reverb, and occasional drops in the arrangement create a sense of space and distance. These sonic choices mimic the act of pulling back, giving the listener a feeling of both intimacy and separation.

**Q: Does the song advocate for avoidance, or is it a critique?**  
A: Rather than prescribing a stance, the track offers a nuanced reflection. It acknowledges the necessity of boundaries while hinting at the loneliness that can result when avoidance becomes a default mode.

**Q: Why do fans feel a strong personal connection to this track?**  
A: The combination of ambiguous lyricism, emotive vocal delivery, and ambient production allows listeners to project their own experiences onto the song, turning it into a personalized narrative of self‑discovery and emotional stewardship.

**Q: Can the song be interpreted as a step toward healing?**  
A: Yes. By openly naming the act of avoidance, the narrator takes a small but significant step toward awareness. This acknowledgment is often the first move in a healing journey, suggesting that the song is both a confession and a quiet proclamation of intent.

**Q: How does “Avoid Things” fit into Tems’s larger artistic narrative?**  
A: The track aligns with Tems’s recurring focus on introspection, vulnerability, and the complexities of emotional navigation. It adds another layer to her catalogue by exploring the paradox of protecting oneself while yearning for connection, a theme that threads through much of her work.
