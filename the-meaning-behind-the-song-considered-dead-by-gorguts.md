The relentless barrage of dissonant riffs and hyper‑technical drumming on **Gorguts’** “Considered Dead” feels at first like a pure assault on the senses, yet beneath the sheer intensity lies a tightly wound meditation on how people are reduced to numbers, statistics, and cold judgments when they no longer fit the expectations of a demanding world. The track does not simply scream about death; it asks the listener to confront the way society **labels** and **exiles** those whose existence is deemed inconvenient. In a genre that often revels in abstraction, Gorguts gives us a lyric narrative that is unmistakably personal—an inner monologue that wrestles with self‑worth, invisibility, and the desperate yearning to be recognized as more than a datum on a ledger. Understanding why the song resonates with fans of extreme metal, as well as with listeners who have ever felt “othered,” demands a close look at its emotional core, the metaphors that saturate its verses, and how the music itself acts as an aural extension of the lyrical theme.

## Key Takeaways
- **“Considered Dead”** portrays the psychological fallout of being socially declared worthless.  
- The narrator’s voice oscillates between resignation and a fierce, almost nihilistic defiance.  
- Symbols such as rust, decay, and “blank pages” illustrate the erosion of identity under bureaucratic scrutiny.  
- The song’s title works as a literal and figurative verdict, reinforcing the notion that existence can be reduced to a status.  
- Gorguts’ jagged production—dissonant chords, abrupt tempo shifts, and layered textures—mirrors the disorientation of people whose lives are constantly re‑evaluated.  
- Fans connect with the track because it translates a universal fear of invisibility into the language of technical death metal, offering catharsis through sonic aggression.  
- The track ultimately questions whether the label of “dead” is an external judgment or an internal acceptance.

## The Emotional Core of “Considered Dead”

### Narrative Voice and Inner Conflict  
The speaker in “Considered Dead” is unmistakably someone who has internalized the verdict of others. The verses are presented in a first‑person monologue that feels like a confession whispered from a corner of a dimly lit interrogation room. The narrator admits to feeling **exhausted** by the perpetual scrutiny—each line is a confession that the world has *already* decided the outcome of his story. This exhaustion is not passive; it carries an undercurrent of **anger**, a spiteful awareness that the result—being “considered dead”—is an imposed reality rather than an inevitable truth.

The conflict arises from two opposing drives: the need to **survive** the label and the temptation to **embrace** it as a means of escape. Throughout the track, there are moments when the narrator almost surrenders, describing an inner “emptiness” that aligns with the imposed death. Yet the crushing weight of those same words is countered by ferocious guitar passages that sound like a desperate attempt to break free, suggesting that the emotional core is a **push–pull between resignation and rebellion**.

## Central Themes and Messages

### Mortality and Existential Neglect  
While the title explicitly references death, the song’s true focus is on **existential neglect**—the feeling that one’s life has been discounted before the natural end arrives. The lyrics evoke a stateless existence: bureaucratic language, cold numerical assessments, and an absence of empathetic human touch. This reflects a broader cultural anxiety about how modern institutions can render individuals *functionally invisible*, treating them as “dead” long before any physical demise.

### Alienation and Identity  
A secondary but equally powerful theme is **alienation**. The narrator is isolated not merely physically but metaphysically, with his sense of self being eroded by external assessments. The recurring motif of “blankness” works as a metaphor for a person stripped of personal narrative—reduced to an empty file. The track implies that identity is a fragile construct, vulnerable to being overwritten by external judgments. In this context, the assertion of being “considered dead” becomes an indictment of how societies **dehumanize** those who no longer serve a purpose.

## Symbolism and Metaphors in the Lyrics

### The Notion of “Dead” as Social Status  
The word *dead* in the title and refrain operates on two levels. First, it conveys the literal finality associated with death. Second, it functions as a **social status**, akin to being *excommunicated* or *cancelled*. By employing this double entendre, Gorguts encourages listeners to question the morality of assigning such verdicts without due process. The lyrical narrative frames “dead” as an external label—a *diagnosis* handed down by a cold, indifferent system.

### Imagery of Decay, Rust, and Erosion  
Throughout the track, visual metaphors such as rust and decay appear. These images reference **corrosion**—the gradual, unstoppable erosion of one’s sense of self when constant criticism and neglect wear away at the core. The rusted metal symbolizes the hardened exterior that develops when the inner self is constantly under attack. By choosing this imagery, the song paints a picture of how an individual, once vibrant, becomes infected by the corrosive forces of alienation.

### “Blank Pages” and the Loss of Narrative  
Another striking metaphor is the reference to “blank pages” that supposedly should have held a story. This signifies a **void** where personal history has been erased, either by self‑destruction or by the erasure imposed by others. In the context of extreme metal, where storytelling is often dense and graphic, the blankness underlines the horror of having one’s tale forgotten entirely. It underscores a feeling of **nihilism**, not from the absence of meaning in the universe, but from the absence of personal meaning granted by others.

## The Title, Hook, and Refrain as a Lens

The hook—repeated declaration that the narrator is “considered dead”—serves as **both a mantra and a curse**. Its repetitive structure functions like a litany that ingrains the label deeper into the psyche, reflecting how repeated societal messages can become internalized. At the same time, the relentless repetition in the music—with cyclical chord progressions—mirrors this mental loop, making the listener experience the **psychological trap** alongside the vocalist.

Unlike a conventional chorus that offers resolution, the refrain here offers no closure; it simply reasserts the label. This artistic choice forces the audience to confront the unsettling possibility that the verdict may be immutable, prompting deeper reflection on whether any act of defiance can truly *unstick* the label once it has been cemented in collective perception.

## Production, Sonic Architecture, and Their Narrative Role

Gorguts is renowned for marrying technical proficiency with avant‑garde ambience, and “Considered Dead” is no exception. The track’s **abrupt tempo shifts** act as a sonic representation of the narrator’s volatile mental state—moments of frantic panic interspersed with slow, crushing passages that simulate the weight of despair. 

The **dissonant chords**—often sliding into microtonal intervals—create an unnerving atmosphere of unease, mirroring the sense of being out of sync with a world that no longer acknowledges you. The layering of **high‑frequency tremolo picking** over lower, guttural growls evokes a feeling of being **overwhelmed by noise**, analogous to the barrage of external judgments that drown out an inner voice.

In production terms, the mix leaves moments of **silence or minimal instrumentation**, only to be punctuated by sudden bursts of wall-of-sound distortion. These quiet interludes serve as auditory *blank pages*, where the listener can feel the void before being thrust back into the chaotic onslaught—mirroring the wavering between anonymity and forced visibility.

## Fan Interpretations and Community Resonance

Among Gorguts’ dedicated following, “Considered Dead” is frequently cited as a **personal anthem for the marginalized**. Many listeners report identifying with the notion of being dismissed by a system that values productivity over humanity. Within underground forums, fans discuss how the track functions as a **collective catharsis**, allowing them to channel frustrations about bureaucratic indifference, mental health stigma, or the feeling of being sidelined in a hyper‑competitive scene.

The song also resonates on a **metal‑specific level**: the genre’s emphasis on confronting darkness makes “Considered Dead” a logical vessel for expressing the dread of being ignored. Fans often note that the technical proficiency provides a sense of **empowerment**, suggesting that despite feeling labeled “dead,” they can master complexity and survive in a hostile environment. This duality—acknowledging vulnerability while showcasing competence—creates a powerful emotional feedback loop that bolsters the track’s status as a seminal piece within Gorguts’ catalog.

## FAQ

**What is the primary meaning behind “Considered Dead” by Gorguts?**  
The song examines how external labels—particularly being declared “dead” before an actual death—strip an individual of identity, turning them into a statistic. It delves into the psychological impact of that dehumanization, exploring both resignation and defiant resistance.

**Is the track about literal death or a metaphorical state?**  
While death is referenced literally, the dominant interpretation is metaphorical. “Dead” functions as a status imposed by society, institutions, or peers, signifying social exclusion and loss of agency rather than physical demise.

**How does Gorguts use musical elements to reinforce the lyrics?**  
Abrupt tempo changes, dissonant chord voicings, and moments of stark silence echo the narrator’s inner turbulence and feelings of erasure. The aggressive riffing mirrors a desperate push against the imposed label, while the occasional calm passages symbolize the existential void.

**Why do fans connect so strongly with the song’s themes?**  
Many fans have experienced personal alienation—whether through mental health struggles, feeling dismissed at work, or being judged by rigid subcultural standards. The track gives voice to that isolation, turning personal pain into a shared, cathartic experience.

**Does the title “Considered Dead” refer to an actual verdict or a self‑fulfilling prophecy?**  
Both. It reflects an external verdict—society’s classification of someone as “dead”—and hints at the danger of internalizing that label, where the person may begin to accept the verdict as truth, turning it into a self‑fulfilling prophecy.

**What role does the refrain play in shaping the song’s narrative?**  
The repeated declaration works as a lyrical mantra, reinforcing the weight of the judgment. Its cyclic nature underscores how persistent labeling can become an inescapable mental loop, compelling listeners to confront the permanence of such verdicts.

**Can “Considered Dead” be interpreted as a critique of modern bureaucracy?**  
Yes; the song’s references to being reduced to numbers, blank pages, and cold assessments point to a broader criticism of bureaucratic systems that prioritize efficiency over humanity, effectively rendering individuals “dead” in a social sense before they physically cease to exist.
