### Symbolism and Metaphors: Decoding the “Bom” and Visual Imagery
The central metaphorical device—the onomatopoeic “bom”—operates on several layers:

1. **Heartbeat** – The most literal interpretation. The thudding sound is a sonic representation of the narrator’s pulse, quickening with attraction and slowing with doubt.  
2. **Explosive Emotion** – “Bom” evokes an explosion, hinting at feelings that are sudden, intense, and potentially damaging. This duality underscores the fear that passion might turn into self‑destruction.  
3. **Digital Notification** – In a hyper‑connected era, a short, sharp sound often indicates an incoming message. This ties the theme to *instantaneous communication* and the perpetual need for validation.  

Visually, the song references neon lights, crowded avenues, and flickering screens. Neon is a **symbol of artificial brightness**—it illuminates but also obscures, reflecting how modern romance can be *bright yet superficial*. Crowded avenues suggest **public exposure**, reinforcing the idea that the narrator’s love life is on display, subject to the eyes of strangers and the pressure of social media.

Another recurring image is the **clock or watch ticking**, which is not mentioned outright but implied through the repetitive rhythm. Time, in this context, becomes a *measuring device* for how quickly feelings intensify and fade. The song subtly asks whether love should be measured in seconds or moments, and whether that measurement is even meaningful.
