### Main Themes and Message

The recurring motif of “not being a certain thing” points to a deeper anxiety about *fixed identity*. The narrator resists being pigeonholed, wrestling with the idea that identity is a fluid, ever‑shifting construct rather than a static label. #### 2. **The Cost of Visibility**
A second theme explores the **price of being seen**. While exposure can bring connection, it also invites scrutiny. The song subtly critiques the modern appetite for "realness" on platforms that reward vulnerability with attention but also with criticism. The lyric about “showing the face that never smiles” encapsulates this trade‑off: a genuine self revealed for the sake of acceptance.

#### 3. **Temporal Dislocation**
Time appears as an undercurrent, manifesting through references to lingering moments and fleeting seconds. The sensation of existing “out of time” mirrors how the narrator feels detached from both the past they left behind and the future they cannot yet conceive. This temporal dislocation is a metaphor for the disorienting pace of contemporary life.

Collectively, these themes converge to convey a message that **embracing uncertainty is both painful and necessary**; the song refuses to offer easy answers, instead urging listeners to sit with the discomfort of unanswered questions.

### Symbolism and Metaphors
#### Water as Fluid Selfhood
Early in the track, the narrator mentions drifting on a “still sea” that later becomes a “storm‑tossed ocean”. Water traditionally symbolizes emotion and change; here it functions as a **metaphor for the narrator’s fluctuating emotional state**. The calm water mirrors moments of peace, while the storm underscores inner turbulence and the fear of being overwhelmed.

#### Mirrors and Reflections
The lyric about “seeing my own eyes in glass that never shows depth” uses mirrors to represent **self‑examination** and the elusive nature of true self‑knowledge. Mirrors are often thought to reflect truth, yet the song suggests that what is reflected is surface‑level—a reminder that introspection can be shallow when we are distracted by external expectations.

#### Broken Clocks and Fragmented Time
References to “broken clocks ticking again” serve as an emblem of **time’s unreliability**. A broken clock that still ticks paradoxically suggests that even broken systems can produce rhythm, hinting that even in disarray there can be a sense of order—a comforting paradox for listeners feeling lost.

These symbols work together to construct a layered narrative where **the external (water, mirrors, clocks) mirrors the internal emotional journey**, allowing listeners to project their own experiences onto the canvas the song paints.

As the song progresses, subtle layers of reverb and echo are added to the vocals, creating an **aura of distance**, as if the singer is both close and far away.

The mixing also places the vocal slightly above the instrumentals, not fully in the foreground, which subtly suggests the narrator’s **struggle to be heard** without overwhelming the surrounding emotional atmosphere.
