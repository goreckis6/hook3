The first time **“Bury The Hatchet”**—featuring Jeremy Tollas—played on a late‑night radio mix, its chorus slipped under the surface of a typical indie‑rock track and settled into something more unsettling. The refrain isn’t a boastful anthem of victory; it feels like a whispered pact, an attempt to lay old conflicts to rest while simultaneously acknowledging that the very act of burying a weapon can stir the soil of memory. For listeners who know Dakota Ave’s catalog, the song feels like a turning point, a moment where the band addresses the tension between forgiveness and the lingering weight of past grievances. This tension gives the track its emotional gravity, inviting anyone who’s ever tried to reconcile with a broken relationship to hear themselves in its layered verses and dense production.

## Key Takeaways
- **The title works as a metaphor for letting go of lingering resentment**; it’s both an act of peace and a reminder that the past isn’t truly erased.  
- **Narrative perspective shifts between accusation and confession**, creating a dual voice that mirrors the push‑and‑pull of an unresolved partnership.  
- **Jeremy Tollas’ guest verses serve as a counter‑point, embodying the “other side” of the dialogue**, adding depth to the theme of mutual accountability.  
- **Production choices—reverb‑drenched guitars, low‑end throbs, and a distant synth line—reflect the hazy, almost subconscious nature of memory**.  
- **Symbolic imagery (soil, rusted metal, weathered roads) points to decay, renewal, and the cyclical nature of conflict**, suggesting that burying one hatchet may simply sow the seed for another.  
- **Fans connect with the song’s sense of ambiguous closure**, resonating with anyone who has tried to “move on” while still feeling the echo of what was left unsaid.

## The Emotional Core of the Song

### A Dialogue in Two Voices

At its heart, **“Bury The Hatchet”** is structured as an intimate conversation. Dakota Ave’s lead vocalist adopts a tone that vacillates between weary resignation and tentative hope. The verses are steeped in a kind of nervous confession—“I’ve carried this weight for too long”—while the chorus lifts into an almost pleading resolve: an earnest desire to lay the fight down and walk forward. Jeremy Tollas’ contribution interrupts this flow with a counter‑tone that feels like a reflective echo, acknowledging his own part in the conflict. The back‑and‑forth creates an emotional *call‑and‑response* that mirrors how two parties often negotiate a fragile truce, each offering half‑truths and half‑blames.

The emotional core thus becomes a **push‑and‑pull between acceptance and regret**. Listeners are invited to feel both the relief of letting go and the lingering ache of what’s been left unsaid. This duality is amplified by the vocal layering in the bridge, where both voices blend into a barely audible hum—suggesting that, beneath the words, there’s an undercurrent of shared sorrow that neither fully acknowledges.

### The Fear of Re‑Opening Old Wounds

Even as the chorus pushes for finality, there’s a subtle undercurrent of fear. The repeated line about “soil that never forgets” alludes to the idea that burying a weapon does not erase its presence; it merely hides it. For anyone who’s tried to move past a toxic relationship, the idea that the past may re‑emerge becomes a palpable anxiety. This tension is reflected in the song’s minor chord progression, which never quite resolves, leaving the listener on an emotional edge—much like the uneasy feeling that a once‑settled conflict can be reignited at any moment.

## Main Themes and Message

### Forgiveness as an Active Choice

Rather than presenting forgiveness as a passive state, **“Bury The Hatchet”** frames it as an active, almost labor‑intensive task. The metaphor of digging, shoveling, and covering a hatchet underscores that letting go involves deliberate effort. The song suggests that true forgiveness isn’t simply “forgetting”; it’s **a conscious decision to cover the past with something that can still be seen but no longer hurts**. This is why the lyric that references “shadows of rusted steel” feels crucial—it conveys that while the weapon may be hidden, its existence still shapes the landscape.

### The Weight of Unspoken History

Another core theme is the *weight* carried by unspoken histories. Dakota Ave’s vocal delivery becomes heavier in the verses that speak about “the silence between us,” a line that can be paraphrased as a shared, unacknowledged history that informs the present. The song insists that any attempt to bury the hatchet must also reckon with the unspoken stories that have accumulated like sediment. By acknowledging this depth, the track urges listeners to confront the truths that linger beneath the surface, rather than ignoring them.

### Ambiguous Closure

A hallmark of the track is its refusal to provide a clear, definitive ending. The final refrain fades out with a lingering synth note that never quite fades away, mirroring the lyrical suggestion that the act of burial doesn’t guarantee permanent peace. Instead, the song delivers **an ambiguous closure**, positioning the act of burying as a step—not the destination. This resonates with the lived reality of many relationships where “moving on” involves ongoing negotiation rather than a single decisive moment.

## Symbolism and Metaphors

### Soil as Memory

The repeated reference to *soil* operates on several symbolic levels. On a literal level, soil is where one buries an object, but metaphorically it represents **the fertile ground of memory**. Just as soil can nurture new growth, it also retains remnants of what was placed within it. This dual nature underscores the song’s tension: the desire to plant something new (peace) while the old (conflict) remains embedded. The imagery of “cold, damp earth” evokes feelings of both comfort—associated with being grounded—and unease, due to its hidden contents.

### Hatchet as Conflict

Choosing a hatchet rather than a sword or a gun signals a more personal, intimate form of conflict. A hatchet is a tool used for chopping wood; it is both constructive and destructive. This duality reflects the way arguments can both clear away old growth and cause painful wounds. By opting to “bury” it, the song suggests an attempt to **transform a weapon of division into a buried relic**, hinting at the possibility of later repurposing (perhaps as a historical reminder rather than an active threat).

### Rust and Weathering

When the lyrics mention a “rusted blade,” the rust serves as a visual metaphor for **the corrosion of time on grievances**. Rust is a slow, inevitable process that weakens metal, showing that unresolved anger inevitably degrades. This image is paired with references to weather—rain, wind—highlighting that natural forces also act upon human emotions, wearing them down but never wholly erasing them.

## The Role of the Title and Hook

The hook—*“let’s bury the hatchet, lay it down, let it rest”*—functions as both a refrain and a mantra. Its simplicity makes it memorable, but its weight lies in the juxtaposition of “bury” (conceal) and “rest” (peace). The title, therefore, operates on *two levels*: a literal instruction and a symbolic statement of intent. By repeating this phrase, Dakota Ave forces listeners to contemplate the desperation behind the act: the need to perform a ritual that marks the end of a cycle, even if the ritual is imperfect.

The song’s structure mirrors this cyclical nature: a loop of verses, chorus, bridge, and a final chorus that never fully resolves. This loop mirrors the idea that burying the hatchet is a *process*, not a single event—each return to the refrain is a re‑affirmation of the commitment to let go.

## How Production and Sound Amplify the Narrative

### Atmospheric Reverb and Space

The production features a **wide, atmospheric reverb** on the vocals, especially in the chorus. This creates a sense of distance, as if the singers are speaking from a field where the buried hatchet lies hidden. The echo feels like an auditory representation of memory reverberating in a cavernous mind—*the farther you move from the source, the more diffused the sound becomes*.

### Low‑End Pulse as a Heartbeat

A subtle, throbbing bass line underpins the track, acting like a **heartbeat** that never stops. This pulse implies that even when we think we have laid conflicts to rest, there is an underlying tension that remains alive. The bass is occasionally syncopated, a gentle nudge that “reminds” the listener that the emotional stakes are not entirely settled.

### Sparse Guitar Textures

The guitars are deliberately sparse, with clean, high‑pitched arpeggios that feel like *shovels digging into earth*. Their occasional distortion during the bridge mimics the frustration of confronting the harder parts of the past. The contrast between clean and distorted tones mirrors the emotional swings from calm acceptance to jagged resentment.

## Fan Resonance and Shared Interpretation

Listeners have gravitated toward **“Bury The Hatchet”** because its lyrics echo the everyday struggle of ending a long‑standing feud, whether romantic, familial, or platonic. Fans often share personal anecdotes of finally deciding to move past a toxic friendship, describing the song as a “soundtrack for the moment I finally put down my own metaphorical hatchet.” The duet with Jeremy Tollas adds a crucial element of *shared accountability*, allowing fans to see themselves as both the one who buries and the one who is buried beneath.

The song also resonates with audiences who appreciate its refusal to give a tidy ending. In a culture that often demands closure, Dakota Ave provides a *realistic portrayal of emotional work*, which many find refreshing. Discussion threads frequently highlight the line about “soil that never forgets,” noting how it captures the feeling that past wounds do not simply vanish when we try to ignore them.

## FAQ

**What does the “hatchet” symbolize in the song?**  
The hatchet stands for the specific conflict or grievance that the narrator wishes to put away. Unlike a sword, a hatchet suggests a more personal, close‑range confrontation—a tool that can both build and destroy, mirroring how arguments can shape relationships.

**Why does the song repeat the phrase “let’s bury the hatchet” instead of offering a final statement?**  
The repetition functions as a mantra, emphasizing the deliberate effort required to let go. It also reflects the cyclical nature of emotional work—each repeat is a renewed commitment rather than a definitive conclusion.

**How does Jeremy Tollas’ verse contribute to the meaning?**  
Tollas’ voice provides the perspective of the “other side,” turning the song into a dialogue. His verses echo the same desire for peace but with an added layer of self‑accountability, reinforcing the theme that reconciliation requires mutual effort.

**Is the song about a romantic breakup, a friendship, or something broader?**  
While the lyrics are intentionally vague, the emotional texture is broad enough to apply to any close relationship strained by unresolved conflict. Its imagery of soil, rust, and weather makes the theme universal, allowing listeners to map their own experiences onto the narrative.

**What does the recurring imagery of soil and rust convey?**  
Soil represents memory and the fertile ground where past events settle; rust signals the slow decay of unresolved anger. Together, they portray how time can both soften and cement old wounds, emphasizing that burying something does not erase its existence.

**How does the production reinforce the lyrical themes?**  
The reverb‑drenched vocals create a sense of distance akin to reflecting on buried memories, while the low‑end pulse mimics an ongoing heartbeat, suggesting that dormant feelings still linger. Sparse, clean guitars act as the “shovel” moving through emotional earth, and the occasional distortion highlights moments of resurfacing tension.

**Why do fans feel the song never fully resolves?**  
The song’s chord progression deliberately avoids a perfect cadence, and the final synth note fades into silence without a clear endpoint. This musical choice mirrors the lyrical message that letting go is an ongoing process, not a one‑time event, resonating with listeners who experience that ambivalence in real life.
