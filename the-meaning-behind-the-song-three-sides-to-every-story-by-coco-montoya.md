The story that **Coco Montoya** tells in “Three Sides To Every Story” feels like a conversation you’re having with a mirror that has learned to speak. From the first chord, the track invites listeners into a space where memory, guilt, and the yearning for redemption collide, suggesting that any conflict we live through is never simply black or white. Montoya’s lyricism hints at a particular personal fracture—perhaps a breakup, a betrayal, or a moment of self‑deception—yet the way he frames it as a triangle of perspectives pushes the narrative beyond a singular experience. It becomes an invitation to examine how we each construct our own version of events, and why those versions often clash, creating the emotional tension that fuels the song’s relentless drive.

---

## Key Takeaways
- **Perspective as a battlefield**: The song argues that truth is fragmented, and each side holds a piece of the whole.
- **Regret as catalyst**: Regret fuels the narrator’s search for reconciliation, but also keeps him trapped in a loop of “what‑ifs.”
- **Metaphorical geometry**: Triangular imagery represents both conflict and the possibility of balance.
- **Production mirrors narrative**: Shifting dynamics in the arrangement echo the changing viewpoints.
- **Universal resonance**: Listeners identify with the feeling of being caught between their own story and the version told by others.

## The Emotional Core of the Song

Montoya’s vocal delivery is steeped in a weary resolve that feels both confessional and confrontational. He oscillates between pleading tones and a steadier, almost resigned, register, suggesting a narrator who has spent a great deal of time replaying a conversation in his head. The underlying fear is not just of losing someone else, but of losing the **self‑image** that has been built around that relationship. The repeated refrain about “three sides” creates a sense of being pulled in multiple directions, each side representing a different emotional weight: love, anger, and longing.  

The core emotion is **heartache tempered by a desire for clarity**. Montoya does not simply mourn a loss; he actively seeks the pieces that make up the fractured memory. This is evident in the lyrical shift from describing a tangible argument to an introspective questioning of the motives behind each participant’s actions. The narrator’s yearning for forgiveness is tinged with a stubborn insistence on truth, exposing a fear that reconciliation might be built on a shaky foundation of half‑truths.

## Main Themes and Message

### 1. **Subjectivity of Truth**
At the heart of “Three Sides To Every Story” is the philosophical claim that reality is **multifaceted**. By explicitly naming three perspectives, Montoya foregrounds the idea that no single narrative can claim absolute authority. This aligns with the broader cultural conversation about “whose story gets told,” prompting listeners to consider how often they have been on the periphery of someone else’s version of events.

### 2. **The Weight of Regret**
Regret surfaces in the verses as a relentless echo that forces the narrator to confront past choices. It is not presented as a simple apology but as a **catalyst for self‑examination**. The song suggests that regret can be constructive when it pushes us to seek a more complete picture rather than simply wallowing in remorse.

### 3. **Search for Balance**
The triangle metaphor, central to the title, is a visual representation of attempting to **balance opposing forces**. While a triangle is inherently stable, its three points also signify tension—any movement at one point ripples through the whole shape. The chorus’s melodic rise mirrors this tension, indicating that balance is achievable only through acknowledging each side’s influence.

### 4. **Identity Reconstruction**
As the narrative progresses, the protagonist realizes that his sense of self has been defined in relation to the other parties involved. The final verses hint at an emerging independence that arises from integrating the three viewpoints, suggesting that the *true* story is one that includes all perspectives, allowing for a redefined identity.

## Symbolism and Metaphors

### Triangular Geometry
The most overt symbol is the triangle, which operates on several levels. Geometrically, a triangle is the simplest polygon that can enclose space, representing **the attempt to contain an otherwise sprawling emotional landscape**. Emotionally, each apex can be read as a different character or facet of the narrator’s psyche: the lover, the betrayer, and the inner critic. By naming the shape, Montoya proposes that these components are distinct yet inseparably linked.

### Light and Shadow
Throughout the track, Montoya references “light flickering behind the curtains” and “shadows that linger after the day is done.” The interplay of illumination and darkness serves as a metaphor for **clarity versus ignorance**. When the “light” is described as fleeting, it underscores how moments of understanding can be temporary, easily obscured by the “shadows” of doubt and memory.

### Roads and Crossroads
Imagery of winding roads leading to a crossroads appears in the bridge, standing in for **choices that branch out from each narrative perspective**. The crossroads is not a place of decision but a place where the three stories converge, emphasizing that the eventual path taken is a synthesis of all three.

## The Role of the Title and Hook

The title, “Three Sides To Every Story,” is more than a clever turn of phrase; it acts as a thematic thesis that frames every lyrical and musical decision. The hook, which repeats the phrase “three sides,” functions as an **anchor** for the listener, constantly reminding them of the song’s central argument. Its repetition also mirrors how, in real life, we often return to the same question when trying to make sense of a conflict: “What really happened?” By embedding the title within the hook, Montoya ensures the concept is not merely intellectual but lived through the song’s cadence.

The hook’s melodic construction—starting on a minor chord, moving through a suspended tension, and resolving briefly before falling back—mirrors the **oscillation between uncertainty and momentary clarity**. This musical mechanic reinforces the lyrical claim that each side offers a different resolution, none of which fully settles the underlying tension.

## How Production and Sound Support the Narrative

The arrangement employs a **layered approach** that mirrors the multiplicity of perspectives. In the verses, the instrumentation is sparse: a clean electric guitar, subtle percussion, and a warm bass line, allowing the vocal to occupy the foreground. This minimalism represents the narrator’s solitary contemplation. As the chorus arrives, additional guitars, backing harmonies, and a swelling drum pattern enter, symbolizing the convergence of the three sides.

The productionist also uses **spatial effects**—reverb that expands during the bridge and tighter, more intimate mixing during the verses—to evoke distance and closeness. When the narrator reflects on each perspective, the soundstage widens, suggesting the mental expansion required to accommodate another’s viewpoint. Conversely, the moments of personal doubt are treated with a narrower, more claustrophobic mix, pulling the listener into the narrator’s internal turmoil.

A subtle, recurring synth pad underneath the chorus adds a **tonal ambiguity**, never fully resolving, which reinforces the unresolved nature of the three‑sided conflict. The final fade‑out—a fading guitar lick that never quite lands on a decisive chord—leaves the listener hanging, echoing the song’s message that absolute resolution may be elusive.

## Fan Interpretation and Why It Resonates

Listeners often point to personal experiences of **relationship fallout** when discussing “Three Sides To Every Story.” Many fans describe how they have felt misunderstood, caught between their own recollection of an event and the narrative painted by a former partner. The song’s insistence that there are always multiple sides validates these feelings, giving a framework to articulate the often‑chaotic emotional aftermath.

Another prevalent interpretation concerns **creative collaboration**—the idea that an artistic project can feel like a three‑sided story when the creator, the audience, and the critics each hold distinct viewpoints. This reading resonates especially with musicians and writers who feel trapped between personal intent and external perception.

What **binds these interpretations** is the universal human need to make sense of conflict. By presenting the story as a triangle rather than a binary opposition, Montoya offers a more nuanced language for participants to discuss pain, blame, and forgiveness. The song becomes a therapeutic mirror, reflecting back the complexity of real life and inviting listeners to consider the pieces they might have omitted in their own narratives.

## FAQ

**Q: What does the “three sides” metaphor specifically refer to?**  
A: It symbolizes the three distinct perspectives involved in any conflict—the narrator’s, the other party’s, and the often‑overlooked internal voice that judges and processes the events. The metaphor emphasizes that each side carries its own truth, shaping a composite reality.

**Q: Is the song about a romantic breakup or something broader?**  
A: While the lyrical details hint at relational intimacy, the underlying theme is broader: any situation where differing narratives clash—friendships, family disputes, or creative partnerships—can be mapped onto the three‑sided framework Montoya presents.

**Q: How does the production enhance the theme of multiple perspectives?**  
A: By layering instruments and altering spatial mixing throughout the track, the arrangement creates auditory “rooms” that represent different viewpoints. The sparse verses convey solitary reflection, while the fuller choruses embody the convergence of multiple narratives.

**Q: Why does Montoya repeat the phrase “three sides” throughout the song?**  
A: The repetition functions as a lyrical anchor, constantly reminding the listener of the central thesis. It also mirrors the mental loop of revisiting each perspective, reinforcing the song’s cyclical emotional pattern.

**Q: What is the significance of the triangle imagery in the bridge?**  
A: The triangle stands for both stability and tension. In geometry it is a sturdy shape, suggesting the potential for balance when all three points are acknowledged. Simultaneously, its sharp angles convey the friction inherent in reconciling differing stories.

**Q: Do fans interpret the song as an apology?**  
A: Some view it as a tentative apology, noting the narrator’s desire for understanding. However, the song stops short of a full confession, instead focusing on the need for mutual acknowledgment rather than unilateral remorse.

**Q: Can the song’s message be applied to societal issues?**  
A: Absolutely. The principle that every story has multiple sides applies to broader discourses—political debates, historical narratives, and cultural conflicts—encouraging listeners to seek a more holistic view before forming judgments.
