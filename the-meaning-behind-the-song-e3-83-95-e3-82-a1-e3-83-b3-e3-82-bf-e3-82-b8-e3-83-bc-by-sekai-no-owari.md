### The Emotional Core of the Song

At its heart, **“%e3%83%95%e3%82%a1%e3%83%b3%e3%82%bf%E3%82%B8%E3%83%BC”** is a confession of a protagonist who feels simultaneously *exhilarated* and *paralyzed*. The verses convey a trembling anticipation, as if the narrator is about to step onto a stage that is both familiar and alien. This duality is reflected in the vocal delivery—soft, almost whisper‑like in moments of introspection, then exploding into a brighter, soaring chorus that feels like a release of built‑up pressure. The emotional trajectory mimics the pattern of a dream: you start in a blurry predawn, become sharply aware of details, and finally wake with a lingering sense of loss.

The narrator’s fear is not just of missing an external goal, but of losing a part of themselves in the process. The recurring line that suggests “the world keeps turning while I stay still” points to **anxiety about personal stagnation**. Yet the song’s uplifting instrumentation refuses to let that fear dominate, proposing instead that the very act of feeling stuck is itself a *catalyst* for change. The emotional conflict thus becomes a springboard: the longing is not a flaw, but the engine driving the narrator forward.

### Main Themes and Message

**1. The Elusiveness of Fantasy** – The title, rendered in Japanese characters, translates to “fantasy.” Throughout the track, “fantasy” is not described as a concrete object but as an ever‑shifting silhouette that appears brighter the farther it recedes. This aligns with the universal idea that our most vivid dreams evolve as we chase them; they are less about a final destination and more about the *process* of pursuit.

**2. Self‑Recognition Through Mirrors** – Imagery of mirrors surfaces repeatedly, functioning as a metaphor for **self‑evaluation**. When the narrator looks into a reflective surface, they see a version of themselves that is both *recognizable* and *distorted* by longing. The mirror becomes a space where hope and doubt meet, prompting listeners to ask: *Which version of me am I truly chasing?*

**3. Light as Hope and Illusion** – The recurring motif of light—dawn, neon, and distant stars—works on two levels. On one hand, it signals *optimism* and the promise of a new beginning. On the other, it can be a *bewildering glare* that masks the true shape of the path ahead. This duality reflects the tension between trusting in one’s dreams and questioning whether they are merely *shimmering distractions*.

**4. The Cycle of Repetition** – Musically, the song cycles through a pattern of verses, pre‑choruses, and choruses that echo one another. Lyrically, the narrator repeats similar sentiments of yearning, hinting at a **psychological loop**: each attempt to move forward feels like a replay of the same emotional script. The message here is subtly hopeful: recognizing the loop is the first step toward breaking it.

### Symbolism and Metaphors

Sekai No Owari’s lyricism often leans on visual metaphors, and this track is no exception. The **“blue horizon”**—a recurring line—is less about geography and more about *emotional distance*. Blue traditionally connotes melancholy, so a horizon painted in that hue suggests a goal that is both beautiful and tinged with sadness. The horizon also represents the *future*, ever‑present but never fully reachable, a perfect rendering of the song’s thematic core.

The **“flight of paper cranes”** metaphor appears as a gentle, almost childlike image. In Japanese culture, paper cranes symbolize wishes for healing and peace. Their fragile construction, however, underlines how *dreams are delicate*—easily torn by harsh winds, yet persistent enough to rise again. This juxtaposition captures the dual nature of hope: fragile, yet resilient.

Another layered metaphor is the **“echo in an empty room.”** The echo represents *reverberated thoughts*—the lingering doubts that bounce back each time the narrator tries to quiet them. The empty room suggests *inner solitude*, reinforcing the introspective tone of the composition. Listeners often interpret this as the moment when the mind becomes a cavern where personal narratives replay, emphasizing the need to break free from self‑imposed loops.

### The Role of the Title and Hook

The song’s hook repeats the phrase “fantasy” in a melodic cadence that climbs and then drops, echoing the feeling of *reaching for an imagined peak only to be pulled back*. This musical device does more than establish a catchy refrain; it **mirrors the emotional climb** that the narrator experiences. The title, therefore, is not merely a label but a *central pillar* of the narrative architecture. By embedding the word within the hook, Sekai No Owari forces the listener to confront the same word repeatedly, pushing it from an abstract concept into a personal affirmation.

### Production, Sound, and Emotional Narrative

The production palette of **“%e3%83%95%e3%82%a1%e3%83%b3%e3%82%bf%E3%82%B8%E3%83%BC”** is deliberately balanced between bright electronic synths and darker, resonant bass lines. The **shimmering arpeggios** at the song’s start invoke the feeling of sunrise—a visual cue for hope. As the track progresses, a low‑frequency pulse emerges, almost like a heartbeat, reminding the audience of the *underlying anxiety* that drives the lyrics.

Layered vocal harmonies add a sense of community, as if multiple versions of the self are singing together, representing internal dialogue. When the chorus swells, the arrangement opens up with soaring strings and a brighter mix, giving the impression of **breaking through a barrier**. Conversely, the bridge strips the instrumentation down to a solitary piano line, reverting to vulnerability and reinforcing the notion that the journey toward one’s fantasy is *punctuated by moments of stark clarity*.

A: The blue horizon blends melancholy with the promise of distance. It captures the yearning for a goal that is visible yet far away, embodying both the beauty and the sadness of continual longing.
